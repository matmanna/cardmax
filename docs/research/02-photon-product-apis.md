# Pointmaxx research 2: Photon, price APIs, UPC extraction (2026-10-22)

## 1. Photon

**Product.** Photon (photon.codes) makes **Spectrum**, an MIT-licensed TypeScript SDK (`spectrum-ts`, for Node or Bun) with a hosted cloud. Channels: iMessage, WhatsApp Business, Telegram, terminal, voice. https://photon.codes/docs/ . Other languages: roadmap only (UNVERIFIED). https://hackernoon.com/introducing-spectrum-agents-for-the-rest-of-us

**Channels.** The main channel is iMessage. Every managed tier includes "RCS and SMS fallback." https://photon.codes/pricing

**Integration.** Messages arrive as an async stream over gRPC or through signed HTTP webhooks. https://photon.codes/docs/ and https://photon.codes/docs/webhooks/overview.md . Cloud mode needs `SPECTRUM_PROJECT_ID` and `SPECTRUM_PROJECT_SECRET` from https://app.photon.codes/ . There is also `@spectrum-ts/imessage-local`, which runs fully locally on a Mac by reading `~/Library/Messages/chat.db`. Needs Full Disk Access, no credentials. https://photon.codes/docs/spectrum-ts/providers/imessage . The `terminal` provider works offline. https://photon.codes/docs/spectrum-ts/getting-started

**Cost and onboarding.** The Free tier is $0 and includes a shared iMessage number, up to 10 users, and SMS/RCS fallback. Pro is $25/mo for 100 users. Business is $250 per line per month for a dedicated number. https://photon.codes/pricing . To get a shared line, add the tester's phone or email in the dashboard and then "Assign a number from the shared pool." The user must text first: "a shared line cannot send an unsolicited first message." https://photon.codes/docs/beta/get-started/imessage.md . On shared lines, each user may see a **different number**, and group chats are not supported. Defaults: 5,000 msgs/server/day, 50 new conversations/line/day. https://photon.codes/docs/spectrum-ts/providers/imessage/connection-and-routing.md . Signup time UNVERIFIED; no approval step documented.

**A2P 10DLC.** iMessage goes through Apple, not the carrier A2P system, so it should avoid 10DLC. That is an inference; Photon does not say it. How Photon handles registration for SMS fallback is UNVERIFIED. The ToS puts carrier compliance and consent on the customer (§3.6) and bans spam (§3.6). https://photon.codes/terms-of-services

**Who reads plaintext.**
- **Photon: yes, in Cloud mode.** iMessage is end to end encrypted only between devices, and "Apple can't decrypt the data." https://support.apple.com/guide/security/imessage-security-overview-secd9764312f/web . In Cloud mode, the receiving "device" is Photon's macOS relay node, so the iMessage encryption ends at Photon's infrastructure. https://photon.codes/blog/how-photon-built-one-of-the-most-stable-and-enterprise-ready-imessage-apis . Photon's "same end-to-end encryption" claim is misleading.
- **Apple:** cannot read the content. It sees routing metadata and queues messages up to 30 days. https://support.apple.com/guide/security/how-imessage-sends-and-receives-messages-sec70e68c949/web
- **Carriers:** can read any message that falls back to SMS. They can likely read RCS too, unless the conversation qualifies for RCS E2EE. Both points are UNVERIFIED.
- **No E2EE from user to bot code**, except self-hosted local Mac mode.

**Retention and privacy.** The privacy policy (updated 2026-09-25) promises TLS in transit and encryption at rest, and says no data is sold. It says data is deleted or anonymized within 30 days of account deletion, but aggregated logs are kept longer. It says nothing specific about message content. https://photon.codes/privacy-policy . The ToS (§12.3) adds a 30-day export window and says data may remain in backups. Under §5.2(e), Usage Data and Aggregated Data may train Photon's ML models. Photon says it does not "review, monitor" message content (§3.6). The ToS also bans automated _financial advice_ "without supervision by a qualified professional and clear disclosure" (§3.8(b)),; frame answers as rewards math. https://photon.codes/terms-of-services

## 3. Reading the UPC/GTIN in the extension

schema.org `Product` supports `gtin`, `gtin12`, `gtin13` and `sku`. A 12-digit UPC plus a leading zero gives a GTIN-13, so you need to normalize. https://schema.org/Product . Google recommends `gtin` in its merchant-listing structured data. https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/merchant-listing

I fetched pages with curl on 2026-10-22:
- **Amazon:** the AirPods page had **no `ld+json` and no UPC anywhere**. Expect to get only the ASIN and title from Amazon.
- **Walmart:** returned a "Robot or human?" bot wall.
- **Home Depot:** returned 403.
- **Best Buy:** timed out.
- **Target, Costco:** my URLs 404'd.

A content script in the user's real tab avoids bot walls, but how often each retailer exposes a GTIN is **UNVERIFIED**. The fallback order should be JSON-LD `gtin*`, then microdata or `og` meta, then retailer-specific embedded JSON such as Walmart's `__NEXT_DATA__` (UNVERIFIED), then title search.
